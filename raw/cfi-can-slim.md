## Overview

CAN SLIM is an investment framework developed by William O'Neil (founder of Investor's Business Daily) designed to identify high-growth stocks through seven key characteristics.

## The seven components

### C — Current Quarterly Earnings
Quarterly EPS must increase by at least **25%** vs the same quarter the previous year. Accelerating EPS growth in recent quarters is a strong positive sign.

### A — Annual Earnings Growth
Annual earnings should demonstrate consistent growth over five years, with yearly EPS expanding at minimum **25% over the past three years**.

### N — New Product, Service, or Management
The company should have catalysts driving future performance — innovative offerings or newly-appointed strong leadership.

### S — Supply and Demand
Stock should show strong trading volume during price increases. Smaller float (fewer outstanding shares relative to peers) is preferred.

### L — Leader or Laggard
Requires "a relative price strength of 80 or higher" — a technical indicator measuring stock performance against the broader market.

### I — Institutional Sponsorship
Increasing number of institutional investors (mutual funds, pension plans, banks, insurance companies) purchasing shares.

### M — Market Direction
"Stock purchases should only be made when the market direction is upwards." Critical criterion — stocks meeting all other criteria can still decline during downturns.

## Application notes

- CAN SLIM identifies **growth stocks** specifically, not value stocks.
- Works best in bullish markets.
- Requires experienced investors — qualifying stocks tend to decline sharply during market reversals.
