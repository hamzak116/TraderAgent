## Definition & Core Concept

Technical analysis involves forecasting future financial price movements by examining past price patterns. It functions similarly to weather forecasting — offering probabilistic rather than absolute predictions about how asset prices will move over time.

## Applicable Assets & Timeframes

This approach applies to any tradable instrument where supply and demand influence pricing: stocks, indexes, commodities, futures, and currencies. Analysis can utilize intraday data (1-minute to hourly), daily, weekly, or monthly price information spanning hours to years.

## Three Key Foundational Assumptions

**Liquidity:** Assets must be heavily traded to enable efficient entry and exit without significant price concessions.

**No Artificial Price Changes:** Adjustments from splits, dividends, or distributions should be corrected in historical data to prevent chart distortion.

**No Extreme News:** Major events (management changes, regulatory shifts, geopolitical developments) temporarily invalidate technical signals until price patterns stabilize around the new baseline.

## Three Pillars from Dow Theory

1. **Price Discounts Everything:** Current market price reflects all available information and represents fair value for analysis purposes.
2. **Price Movements Are Not Totally Random:** Markets experience extended random periods interrupted by identifiable non-random trends and patterns.
3. **"What" Supersedes "Why":** Focus on current price and historical price movement rather than underlying fundamental reasons.

## Core Analysis Framework

- **Overall Trend Identification:** Employ trend lines, moving averages, or peak/trough analysis to determine if price moves upward, downward, or sideways.
- **Support Levels:** Congestion areas and previous lows below the current price mark the support levels.
- **Resistance Levels:** Congestion areas and previous highs above the current price mark resistance levels.
- **Momentum:** Measured via oscillators like MACD to assess bullish or improving conditions.
- **Buying/Selling Pressure:** Volume-based indicators (Chaikin Money Flow) reveal which force dominates.
- **Relative Strength:** Price divided by benchmark (typically S&P 500 for stocks) shows outperformance or underperformance.

## Top-Down Methodology

1. Analyze broad market indices (S&P 500, NASDAQ, Dow)
2. Evaluate sector performance within broader market context
3. Select individual stocks from strongest sectors

## Key Strengths

- **Leads Fundamental Developments:** Price movements typically precede underlying business changes
- **Supply/Demand Clarity:** Open, high, low, close data visually represents competing forces
- **Entry Point Timing:** Identifies optimal trade initiation near support or during breakouts
- **Accessible Historical Record:** Charts provide intuitive visualization of volatility, volume patterns, and relative performance

## Notable Limitations

- **Subjective Interpretation:** Two technicians analyzing identical charts may reach different conclusions
- **Timing Delays:** Significant price moves often complete before trend confirmation arrives
- **Signal Unreliability:** Patterns and indicators lack universal application; effectiveness varies by security
- **Qualified Stances:** Technicians frequently qualify bullish/bearish views with additional conditions and levels

## Critical Perspective

The field treats markets as "80% psychological and 20% logical," emphasizing behavioral and sentiment factors alongside rational valuation mechanics.
