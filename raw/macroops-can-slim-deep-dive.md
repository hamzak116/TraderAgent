## Seven components with quantitative specifics

### C — Current earnings
Target quarterly EPS growth of **"at least 20 to 50 percent year to year."** Historical data showed top performers averaged **70% earnings increases** before major advances.

### A — Annual earnings
Seek minimum **5-year compounded annual growth rate of 24%**. O'Neil recommends "annual EPS that has increased for the last 3 consecutive years."

### N — New products, management, or markets
"**95 percent of the greatest winners had something new**" in these categories. Notably: "what seems too high usually goes higher," referring to stocks making new price highs.

### S — Supply and demand (shares outstanding)
Companies should have "**less than twenty-five million shares of capitalization**," with an average of **11.8 million shares** among top performers.

### L — Leader or laggard
Select stocks with high relative strength rankings: "I tend to restrict purchases to companies with **relative strength ranks above 80**."

### I — Institutional sponsorship
Some institutional interest is beneficial, though excessive ownership can create vulnerability to sudden selling.

### M — Market direction
"**Three out of four stocks** will go in the same direction as a significant move in the market averages," making broad market uptrends essential.
