## Economic rationale per factor

### Value
Focuses on purchasing assets at reasonable or discounted prices relative to cash flows. "The price paid matters enormously" over long horizons — buying at elevated valuations leaves little room for upside surprises; purchasing at depressed valuations provides margin of safety and potential for price normalization.

### Quality
Metrics include profitability, balance-sheet strength, and prudent capital allocation. Assesses a company's ability to sustain and grow economic value. High-quality firms demonstrate greater resilience during downturns and reduced dependence on external financing.

Quality acts as a **conditioning variable** rather than a substitute for value — improving confidence that low prices reflect genuine mispricing rather than fundamental deterioration.

### Momentum
Captures the tendency for recently strong performers to continue outperforming near-term. Rationale: "prices adjust gradually because information diffuses slowly, investors underreact to new data, and institutional constraints delay repositioning."

## Complementary nature

The three signals are economically distinct and imperfectly correlated. They "respond differently to macroeconomic conditions, investor sentiment, and business cycles. When one signal is temporarily weak, another often provides support."

Key interaction findings:

### Value + Quality
Among cheap stocks, the highest-quality quintile achieved the highest returns, lowest volatility, and highest Sharpe ratio — with "lower drawdown during the global financial crisis."

### Value + Momentum
Momentum reduces volatility substantially within value portfolios. Deep value portfolios carry substantial anti-momentum bias; incorporating momentum adjusts for this, reducing volatility across the entire value quintile spectrum.

## Implementation approaches

### Multi-dimensional value
Rather than single metrics, the process combines: sales, cash flow, book value, intangibles, dividends, and buybacks — ensuring valuations appear attractive across multiple dimensions simultaneously.

### Unrecognized Value refinement
Incorporates risk adjustments through a signal that reduces exposure to securities whose returns are explained primarily by common factor betas, reallocating toward idiosyncratic pricing anomalies.

### Integration vs. stacking
Key distinction:
- **Stacking** — maintain separate value and quality portfolios, weight them together
- **Integration** — combine signals to select securities simultaneously attractive across dimensions

Integration prevents allocating to expensive high-quality companies or to cheap low-quality stocks within concentrated strategies.

## Rationale for excluding other factors

- **Dividends/dividend growth**: Already embedded within diversified value. Rapidly rising dividends may signal limited organic growth confidence.
- **Volatility / low-vol strategies**: Defensive characteristics emerge organically through quality and valuation; no need to target as separate objective.
- **Size premium**: Research found "no reliable premium to size." Size is a consequence, not a fundamental decision driver.
- **Other signals**: Maintain disciplinary parsimony; add signals only when "clear and distinct economic rationales" exist.

## Process philosophy

Overarching: build a process that is "robust across environments, resilient to behavioral pitfalls, and grounded in economic intuition" — rather than relying on any single signal.
